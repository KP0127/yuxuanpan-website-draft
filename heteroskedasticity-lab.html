Step 1 of 4

Spot it

Assumption MLR.5 (homoskedasticity) says Var(u | x₁, …, x_k) = σ². Knowing x tells you nothing about how noisy y will be. Hetero means different and skedasticity refers to the spread of the errors, so heteroskedasticity means the error variance changes across observations.

Informal check: residual SD within slices of x

Different numbers hint at heteroskedasticity, just like comparing the SD of cigarettes smoked with and without a restaurant smoking ban. The check is informal because it cannot tell you whether the gap is statistically significant. That is what Step 3 is for.

Step 2 of 4

Why it matters

  • OLS is still unbiased and consistent. Unbiasedness only needs MLR.1 to MLR.4; the error variance plays no role.
  • The usual variance formula is wrong, so the usual standard errors (SEs), t tests, F tests, and confidence intervals are invalid.
  • OLS is no longer BLUE (Best Linear Unbiased Estimator). A weighted estimator can be more precise.

Do not take that on faith. The simulation below draws many fresh samples from the data generator, runs OLS on each, and tests the true null H₀: β₁ = 1.5 at the 5% level. A valid test should reject about 5% of the time. Rejecting more often means a Type I error: calling something significant when it is not.

Step 3 of 4

Test it

Under H₀ of homoskedasticity, E(u² | x) = σ², so the squared residuals should not move with x. Both tests regress û² on functions of x and ask whether that regression explains anything.

    Significance level
    Click a card to plot its statistic.

    Road map. If the test does not reject, the usual OLS output is fine. If it rejects, either keep OLS and report heteroskedasticity-robust SEs, or switch to Weighted Least Squares (WLS), which is more efficient when the variance model is right. Many applied economists skip the test and report robust SEs from the start, because these tests can have low power in small samples.

    Why down-weight noisy points?

    You measure someone's height 10 times: 5 times with a laser (SD 0.1 cm) and 5 times with a ruler (SD 2 cm). Should each reading count equally?

    Robust SEs

    Keep the OLS coefficients and replace the variance formula with the sandwich estimator, which uses each ûᵢ² in place of a common σ². Valid for heteroskedasticity of unknown form in large samples. In Stata, add , vce(robust).

    WLS with known h(x)

    If Var(u | x) = σ²·h(x), divide every term by √h. The transformed error is homoskedastic, so OLS on the transformed model is BLUE. That is the same as minimizing Σ ûᵢ² / hᵢ.

    Feasible GLS

    When h(x) is unknown, Feasible Generalized Least Squares (FGLS) estimates it: regress log(û²) on the x's, take ĥ = exp(fitted), then run WLS with weights 1/ĥ. The exp() guarantees positive variances. If the variance model may be wrong, report robust SEs for WLS too.

    Transformations can also help. When the spread grows with the level of y, modeling log(y) often removes most of the funnel. Box-Cox transformations generalize this idea by searching for the power transformation that makes the data closest to linear, normal, and homoskedastic.

    Replace y and x1 x2 x3 with your own variables. Note that plain estat hettest tests against the fitted values; the rhs iid options give the n·R² version from the textbook.

    * 1. Estimate by OLS and look at the residuals
reg y x1 x2 x3
rvfplot, yline(0)

* 2. Breusch-Pagan test, LM = n*R-squared version
estat hettest, rhs iid

* 3. White test (levels, squares, and interactions)
estat imtest, white

* Breusch-Pagan by hand
predict uhat, resid
gen uhatsq = uhat^2
reg uhatsq x1 x2 x3
display "LM = " e(N)*e(r2) "   p = " chi2tail(e(df_m), e(N)*e(r2))
    * 4a. Fix: heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors
reg y x1 x2 x3, vce(robust)

* 4b. Fix: WLS when Var(u|x) = sigma^2 * h is known (here h = inc)
reg y x1 x2 x3 [aweight = 1/inc]

* 4c. Fix: feasible GLS
reg y x1 x2 x3
predict u, resid
gen logu2 = ln(u^2)
reg logu2 x1 x2 x3
predict ghat, xb
gen hhat = exp(ghat)
reg y x1 x2 x3 [aweight = 1/hhat]
reg y x1 x2 x3 [aweight = 1/hhat], vce(robust)   // if h may be misspecified
